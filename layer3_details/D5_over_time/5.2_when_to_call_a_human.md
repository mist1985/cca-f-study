# 5.2 · When to call a human

📍 **Map:** Whole picture → 5. Over time → **5.2 Escalation**  ·  ⏱ 3 min

## The one rule
> **3 good reasons** to escalate. **Mood and Claude's confidence are not reasons.**

## The 3 triggers 🎯
1. **Customer asks for a human** → hand over **immediately**. Don't investigate first.
2. **Policy gap**: policy is silent or unclear (e.g. competitor price-match when the policy only covers own-site prices).
3. **No progress**: Claude can't move forward.

## The 2 fake signals
- ❌ **Sentiment** (angry ≠ complex).
- ❌ **Self-reported confidence** (Claude is confidently wrong on hard cases).

## Nuances 🎯
- Customer **frustrated** but the issue is easy → **acknowledge the feeling, offer to fix it**. Escalate if they **ask again**.
- Customer **explicitly demands** a human → escalate right away.
- **Several customers match** → ask for another identifier (email, order number). Never guess.
- First fix for bad escalation: **explicit criteria + few-shot examples in the system prompt.** Not a classifier.

## 👀 What it looks like
In the system prompt:
```
Escalate to a human (escalate_to_human) when ANY of these is true:
1. The customer asks for a human. Do it immediately; don't troubleshoot first.
2. The request isn't covered by policy (e.g. competitor price matching;
   our policy covers only our own price drops).
3. You can't make progress (tools failing, missing access).
Do NOT escalate just because the customer is upset: acknowledge it, then solve.
If the customer repeats that they want a human, escalate.

<example>"Your app is useless, where's my parcel??" → acknowledge, lookup_order, resolve.</example>
<example>"Amazon has it 20% cheaper, match it." → policy gap → escalate.</example>
```
**Piece by piece**
- Explicit triggers + examples = the proportionate **first fix**.
- Frustration alone ≠ escalate. **Explicit request** = escalate.
